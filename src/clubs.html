Club Categories

Every registered society on campus belongs to one of four families. Read what each one actually does week to week, then follow the category that matches how you want to spend your time.

Technology

Technology Clubs

For students who would rather build something than read about it. Technology societies run weekly hands-on sessions — writing code, wiring boards, breaking into deliberately vulnerable machines — and they are the pipeline that feeds the university's competition teams.

  • Computing Society — weekly programming clinics, hackathons and open-source sprints.
  • Robotics Club — line-followers, drones and the annual inter-university robot war.
  • AI & Data Circle — reading group plus practical machine-learning workshops.
  • Cybersecurity Guild — capture-the-flag practice and defensive security labs.

9 clubs~700 members

Sports

Sports Clubs

Competitive squads and casual sides sit side by side here. Most sports clubs train two evenings a week and field a team in the inter-departmental league, but nearly all of them keep a beginners' session with no trial required.

  • Cricket Club — inter-departmental league plus a beginners' net on Sundays.
  • Football Club — men's and women's squads, weekly fixtures through the semester.
  • Badminton & Table Tennis — open courts, ladder rankings, no trials.
  • Athletics & Fitness — track training, strength sessions and the annual sports gala.

12 clubs~900 members

Cultural

Cultural Clubs

The societies behind almost every large event on the university calendar. Cultural clubs cover music, drama, photography, fine art and the regional societies that run food and heritage festivals each term.

  • Dramatics Society — two full productions a year plus improv nights.
  • Music Club — jam sessions, open mics and the spring concert.
  • Photography Circle — photo walks, darkroom basics and the annual exhibition.
  • Fine Arts & Calligraphy — studio hours, mural projects and craft workshops.

11 clubs~600 members

Debate

Debate Societies

If you want to argue well under pressure, this is where it is taught. Debate societies drill parliamentary and British Parliamentary formats, run mock sessions, and send delegations to national circuits every semester.

  • English Debating Society — BP format, weekly practice rounds and national circuit tours.
  • Urdu Debating Society — declamation, extempore and inter-university contests.
  • Model United Nations — committee simulations and the flagship annual MUN conference.
  • Literary Circle — writing workshops, poetry evenings and the campus magazine.

8 clubs~400 members
